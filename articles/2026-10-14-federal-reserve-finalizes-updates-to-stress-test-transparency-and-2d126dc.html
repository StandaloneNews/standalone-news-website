Federal Reserve Finalizes Updates to Stress Test Transparency and Capital Requirements

The Federal Reserve has finalized two rules aimed at increasing transparency and reducing volatility in capital requirements for large banks. These changes include new public input processes for stress test scenarios and a revised method for calculating capital buffers, while a separate proposal seeks feedback on modeling fee income.

The Federal Reserve will now seek annual public feedback regarding the hypothetical scenarios and model changes used in its stress testing process.

For banks with significant trading operations, the agency will apply two global market shock components annually and utilize the one resulting in higher losses to determine test outcomes.

To stabilize capital requirements, the agency will calculate stress capital buffers by averaging the results from the two most recent annual supervisory stress tests.

The agency is seeking public comment on a new model for projecting noninterest income to better account for the different ways banks generate fee income.
